# Datasets

All homework uses small, **synthetic** datasets stored in your repository. They imitate the shape of professional
financial data, but every number is generated by a computer, so nothing here is licensed market data. Tickers and company
names are fictional.

The data is **clean**: every key is unique, there are no missing values (NULLs) and no duplicate rows. Every table can be
joined on `ticker`.

| Table | File in your repository | Rows | One row per | Key |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `prices` | `data/equity_prices.csv` | 984 | ticker per trading day | `ticker`, `trade_date` |
| `companies` | `data/companies.csv` | 10 | company | `ticker` |
| `fundamentals` | `data/fundamentals.csv` | 60 | company per fiscal quarter | `ticker`, `fiscal_year`, `fiscal_quarter` |

## `prices` (from Assignment 1)

8 tickers x 123 trading days, 2025-01-02 to 2025-06-30.

| Column | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `ticker` | text | Fictional symbols: ALPH, BRVO, CRST, DLTA, ECHO, FNCH, GRNT, HLIO |
| `trade_date` | date | Trading day |
| `open_price` | decimal(10,2) | First price of the day |
| `high_price` | decimal(10,2) | Highest price of the day |
| `low_price` | decimal(10,2) | Lowest price of the day |
| `close_price` | decimal(10,2) | Last price of the day |
| `volume` | integer | Shares traded |

## `companies` (from Assignment 3)

One row for each of 10 companies: the 8 in `prices`, plus **IOTA** and **JUNO**. Those two went public after June 2025
(see `listing_date`), so they have no rows in `prices`. That's why an `INNER JOIN` and a `LEFT JOIN` between `companies`
and `prices` give different results.

| Column | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `ticker` | text | Trading symbol (unique) |
| `company_name` | text | Company name |
| `sector` | text | Broad sector, e.g. `Information Technology`, `Health Care` |
| `industry` | text | Narrower industry within the sector |
| `exchange` | text | `NYSE` or `NASDAQ` |
| `hq_state` | text | Two-letter US state of headquarters |
| `listing_date` | date | Date the stock started trading |
| `employees` | integer | Number of employees |

## `fundamentals` (from Assignment 3)

Quarterly financial statements for all 10 companies, six quarters each: fiscal 2024 Q1 to fiscal 2025 Q2. Every company's
fiscal year matches the calendar year. **Money amounts are in millions of US dollars** and share counts are in millions.

| Column | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `ticker` | text | Trading symbol (matches `companies.ticker`) |
| `fiscal_year` | integer | Fiscal year, 2024 or 2025 |
| `fiscal_quarter` | integer | 1 to 4 |
| `period_end_date` | date | Last day of the quarter (Mar 31, Jun 30, Sep 30, Dec 31) |
| `report_date` | date | Date the results were published (a weekday, about 5 weeks after quarter end, about 10 weeks for Q4) |
| `revenue` | decimal(12,1) | Sales for the quarter, $ millions |
| `net_income` | decimal(12,1) | Profit for the quarter, $ millions (negative = a loss) |
| `eps` | decimal(8,2) | Earnings per share for the quarter, $ (`net_income / shares_outstanding`) |
| `shares_outstanding` | decimal(10,1) | Shares outstanding, millions |
| `total_assets` | decimal(12,1) | At quarter end, $ millions |
| `total_debt` | decimal(12,1) | At quarter end, $ millions |
| `total_equity` | decimal(12,1) | Shareholders' equity at quarter end, $ millions |

Things to keep in mind when joining:

- `prices` covers January to June 2025. The fiscal 2024 Q4 and 2025 Q1 reports came out inside that window; earlier
  quarters were reported before it, and 2025 Q2 after it.
- Market cap in $ millions is `close_price * shares_outstanding` (both tables needed).
- IOTA and JUNO report losses, so their `net_income` and `eps` are negative.

Later assignments add tables for foreign exchange and bonds/yields.
Class demonstrations may also use WRDS (the university's research data service); homework never requires it.
